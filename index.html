About

I am an applied environmental economist completing my PhD at Africa Business School, University Mohammed VI Polytechnic in Morocco, under a joint doctoral agreement with the Programa de Engenharia de Produção at COPPE, Federal University of Rio de Janeiro, Brazil. My doctorate is formally in Management and Organizational Sciences, supervised by Prof. Amine Ben Amar (Finance Cluster, ABS) and Prof. Olga Gorelkina (Moroccan Center for Game Theory), with Prof. Luan Santos (COPPE, UFRJ) as co-supervisor. My dissertation examines how climate shocks, carbon pricing failures, and fossil-fuel underpricing transmit through financial systems, sovereign debt markets, and global value chains across both advanced and emerging economies.

My research covers fossil-fuel underpricing and the structural transformation of global value chains, sovereign CDS dynamics and climate vulnerability, climate risk transmission through food manufacturing in the UK, the decomposition of carbon price deficits across 174 countries, household coping with climate shocks across ten African countries, the financial adaptation capacity of Nigerian non-farm enterprises, and corporate bond spreads in emerging markets. I employ advanced econometric methods including SVAR with recursive identification, time-varying local projections with stochastic volatility, wavelet coherence analysis, triple fixed effects panels, within-household first differences, and event study designs.

Before pursuing doctoral studies, I spent about three years at OCP Africa in Nigeria as a Regional Field Project Manager and Research Agronomist, where I led agricultural development initiatives in partnership with USAID/TradeHub. I also founded AgriBox SARL, a climate-smart hydroponic enterprise in Morocco.

Research

Job Market Paper  ·  Under Review  ·  Structural Change and Economic Dynamics

Fossil Fuel Underpricing Blocks Disaster-Induced Structural Transformation: Evidence from Carbon-Intensive Global Value Chains

Uke, O. D.

Using a country-sector-year panel of 77 countries, 18 manufacturing sectors, and seven years (2016–2022) under triple fixed effects, I show that fossil-fuel underpricing blocks the reallocation away from carbon-intensive sectors that climate disasters would otherwise induce. The marginal effect of a climate disaster on high-carbon GVC output flips sign at an underpricing threshold of 1.845% of GDP (68.5th percentile), and 31.5% of country-sector-year cells sit on the lock-in side of that threshold. Lock-in concentrates in fossil-fuel exporters, non-high-income countries, and weak-governance settings, and the result holds in all 77 leave-one-country-out estimates.

Revise and Resubmit  ·  International Review of Financial Analysis

Riding the Climate Wave: A Wavelet Analysis of Sovereign Risk in Developed and Developing Economies

Uke, O. D., Ben Amar, A., Gorelkina, O.

Which dimension of climate risk do sovereign credit markets price, physical exposure or the capacity to adapt? Using 59 economies (28 advanced, 31 EMDE) over 2008–2021, wavelet coherence shows co-movement between climate vulnerability and CDS spreads across short, medium and low frequencies, neither concentrated in the long horizon nor systematically different between advanced economies and EMDEs. In the cross-section the vulnerability gradient vanishes once development is controlled: what is priced is readiness. For EMDEs, closing the readiness gap relative to advanced economies is associated with about 74% smaller spreads, net of income, public debt and governance. We also document and correct a measurement convention that inflates conventional coherence summaries.

Climate Shocks and Food Manufacturing: Propagation, Persistence, and Structural Change in the United Kingdom

Uke, O. D., Ben Amar, A., Gorelkina, O.

Presented at the 3rd QFDAM Conference, Rabat, June 2026. Using UK quarterly data for 1990–2025, we separate primary agriculture from downstream food manufacturing and combine a VARX baseline with time-varying local projections with stochastic volatility. A one-standard-deviation temperature shock raises agricultural output by about 1.1%, while food-manufacturing estimates turn negative, peaking near −0.24% and staying negative for three years, though not individually significant at conventional levels. The time-varying estimates show food manufacturing’s domestic climate sensitivity weakening from the late 2010s, consistent with UK food-import dependence rising from 38% to over 50%. Measured domestic temperature sensitivity has shifted upstream to farming.

Submitted  ·  Energy Policy

Fiscal Capacity, Not Climate Exposure: Decomposing Global Fossil-Fuel Underpricing and Its Equity Burden

Uke, O. D., Gorelkina, O., Ben Amar, A.

Only Sweden, Norway and Finland price carbon above zero once subsidies are netted out; the mean effective carbon price across 174 countries is −$88 per tonne of CO₂. We construct a near-global panel of effective carbon prices for 174 countries (2015–2022) and decompose the underpricing shortfall into a domestic wedge, made up of supply-cost gaps, foregone consumption taxes and local air pollution, and a global climate wedge. Fiscal capacity predicts the domestic wedge, which is roughly two thirds of the total, while no observable state characteristic predicts the climate wedge. Climate vulnerability does not raise deficits directly, yet vulnerability and weak fiscal capacity coincide so closely that the income-normalised burden is about twenty times higher in the most vulnerable quintile. Replication package.

Submitted  ·  World Development

Financial Reach and Household Coping under Climate Shocks: Evidence from Ten African Countries

Uke, O. D., Cort, T. (Yale School of Management)

Harmonised shock-and-coping modules from ten African countries allow a within-household comparison of responses to a covariate climate shock and to an idiosyncratic one. Covariate shocks are associated with a 1.9 to 2.2 percentage-point higher probability of reducing consumption, while idiosyncratic shocks more often trigger asset sales. In the 2018 West African sample, a sender elsewhere in the country is associated with a 4.6 percentage-point smaller shift towards cutting consumption than a same-region sender, and survives administrative-region fixed effects; credit access is statistically indistinguishable from zero. Growing-season rainfall anomalies stay correlated across roughly 254 km, which motivates the reach measure without identifying it. The estimates are associational, and the point is that instrument ownership alone is an incomplete measure of climate resilience: the exposure of the income backing a claim matters alongside the instrument a household holds.

Work in Progress  ·  Target: Business Strategy and the Environment

External Carbon Pricing and Transition Risk in Emerging Markets: Evidence from African Exporters under the EU Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism

Uke, O. D.

On 2025 trade, African exporters face a CBAM liability of EUR 45 million in 2026, rising to EUR 1.80 billion at full phase-in in 2034. The ad valorem equivalent rises with processing stage in all four covered sectors, reaching 48.2% on semi-finished steel against a zero most-favoured-nation tariff, so the mechanism reintroduces the tariff escalation that trade policy removed. Exposure also resolves to individual installations: in several countries one production line carries 88% to 98% of national liability, which makes sovereign and single-firm transition risk the same object. Built from an exporter-by-tariff-line measure combining the Commission's country-specific default values, Eurostat Comext trade at CN8 and the certificate price.
