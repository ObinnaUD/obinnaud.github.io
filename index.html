About

I am an applied environmental and financial economist completing my PhD at Africa Business School, University Mohammed VI Polytechnic in Morocco, under a joint doctoral agreement with Decision Sciences, Production Engineering, at COPPE, Federal University of Rio de Janeiro, Brazil. My doctorate is formally in Management and Organizational Sciences (Sustainable Finance), supervised by Prof. Amine Ben Amar (Finance Cluster, ABS) and Prof. Olga Gorelkina (Moroccan Center for Game Theory), with Prof. Luan Santos (COPPE, UFRJ) as co-supervisor. My dissertation examines how climate shocks, carbon pricing failures, and fossil-fuel underpricing transmit through financial systems, sovereign debt markets, and global value chains across both advanced and emerging economies.

My research covers fossil-fuel underpricing and the structural transformation of global value chains, sovereign CDS dynamics and climate vulnerability, climate risk transmission through food manufacturing in the UK, the decomposition of carbon price deficits across 174 countries, the insurability of covariate climate risk for African households, the financial adaptation capacity of Nigerian non-farm enterprises, and corporate bond spreads in emerging markets. I employ advanced econometric methods including SVAR with recursive identification, time-varying local projections with stochastic volatility, wavelet coherence analysis, triple fixed effects panels, within-household first differences, and event study designs.

Before pursuing doctoral studies, I spent over three years at OCP Africa in Nigeria as a Regional Field Project Manager and Research Agronomist, where I led agricultural development initiatives in partnership with USAID/TradeHub. I also founded AgriBox SARL, a climate-smart hydroponic enterprise in Morocco.

Research

Job Market Paper  ·  Under Review  ·  Structural Change and Economic Dynamics

Fossil Fuel Underpricing Blocks Disaster-Induced Structural Transformation: Evidence from Carbon-Intensive Global Value Chains

Uke, O. D.

Using a country-sector-year panel of 77 countries, 18 manufacturing sectors, and seven years (2016–2022) under triple fixed effects, I show that fossil-fuel underpricing blocks the reallocation away from carbon-intensive sectors that climate disasters would otherwise induce. The marginal effect of a climate disaster on high-carbon GVC output flips sign at an underpricing threshold of 1.845% of GDP (68.5th percentile), and 31.5% of country-sector-year cells sit on the lock-in side of that threshold. Lock-in concentrates in fossil-fuel exporters, non-high-income countries, and weak-governance settings, and the result holds in all 77 leave-one-country-out estimates.

Revise and Resubmit  ·  International Review of Financial Analysis

Riding the Climate Wave: A Wavelet Analysis of Sovereign Risk in Developed and Developing Economies

Uke, O. D., Ben Amar, A., Gorelkina, O.

Using 59 countries over 2008–2021, we show that climate vulnerability carries a large cross-country premium in sovereign CDS spreads, and that neither development status nor adaptive capacity insulates a sovereign against it. Wavelet coherence documents the time-frequency structure of the co-movement, which is pervasive across horizons and survives an AR(1) red-noise null.

Climate Shocks, Sectoral Transmission, and Monetary Policy: Evidence from Food Manufacturing in the United Kingdom

Uke, O. D., Ben Amar, A., Gorelkina, O.

Presented at the 3rd QFDAM Conference, Rabat, June 2026. Using an eight-variable SVAR over 1990Q1–2025Q3, together with local projections and time-varying local projections with stochastic volatility, we show that food manufacturing contributes 1.4× more than agriculture to inflation forecast error variance, and that this contribution rose 13% between subsamples while agriculture's fell 93%. Central banks tracking climate-driven food inflation should watch downstream manufacturing rather than domestic agriculture.

Submitted  ·  Energy Policy

Fiscal Capacity, Not Climate Exposure: Decomposing Global Fossil-Fuel Underpricing and Its Equity Burden

Uke, O. D., Gorelkina, O., Ben Amar, A.

We construct a near-global panel of effective carbon prices for 174 countries (2015–2022) and decompose the underpricing shortfall into a domestic wedge, made up of supply-cost gaps, foregone consumption taxes and local air pollution, and a global climate wedge. Fiscal capacity predicts the domestic wedge, which is roughly two thirds of the total, while no observable state characteristic predicts the climate wedge. Climate vulnerability does not raise deficits directly, yet vulnerability and weak fiscal capacity coincide so closely that the income-normalised burden is about twenty times higher in the most vulnerable quintile. Replication package.

Submitted  ·  World Development

Financial Adaptation Capacity and the Insurability of Covariate Climate Risk: Evidence from African Households

Uke, O. D., Cort, T. (Yale School of Management)

Using a within-household design across shock types in the EHCVM surveys of seven WAEMU states, with replication in the Malawi, Uganda and Nigeria LSMS-ISA panels, we show that the insurability of a climate shock depends on the correlation between a household's financial support and the shock, not on the size of that support. Covariate climate shocks fall on the consumption margin, and only support reaching from beyond the roughly 200 km rainfall covariance radius, such as remittances and mobile money, insures against them. Local credit does not. Adaptation capacity is therefore plural: rural households rely on remittances, urban households on mobile money.
