About

I am a PhD candidate in Management and Organizational Sciences (Sustainable Finance) at Africa Business School, University Mohammed VI Polytechnic in Morocco, supervised by Prof. Amine Ben Amar (Finance Cluster, ABS) and Prof. Olga Gorelkina (Moroccan Center for Game Theory). My dissertation examines how climate shocks, carbon pricing failures, and fossil-fuel underpricing transmit through financial systems, sovereign debt markets, and global value chains across both advanced and emerging economies.

My research spans seven papers covering fossil-fuel underpricing and GVC lock-in, sovereign CDS dynamics, monetary policy transmission through food manufacturing in the UK, carbon price deficits and fiscal equity across 174 countries, microfinance and climate resilience in Sub-Saharan Africa, and corporate bond spreads in emerging markets. I employ advanced econometric methods including SVAR with recursive identification, time-varying local projections with stochastic volatility, wavelet coherence analysis, triple fixed effects panels, and bootstrapped mediation analysis.

Before pursuing doctoral studies, I spent over three years at OCP Africa in Nigeria as a Regional Field Project Manager and Research Agronomist, where I led agricultural development initiatives in partnership with USAID/TradeHub. I also founded AgriBox SARL, a climate-smart hydroponic enterprise in Morocco.

Research

Job Market Paper  ·  Submitted

When Climate Stress Does Not Reallocate: Fossil-Fuel Underpricing and Carbon-Intensive Global Value Chains

Uke, O. D.

Submitted to Ecological Economics. Using a country-sector-year panel of 77 countries, 18 manufacturing sectors, and seven years (2016–2022) under triple fixed effects, I show that fossil-fuel underpricing buffers carbon-intensive GVC sectors against climate-disaster-driven reallocation. The marginal effect of a climate disaster on high-carbon GVC output flips sign at an underpricing threshold of 1.845% of GDP (68.5th percentile). The result holds in all 77 leave-one-country-out estimates.

Under Review

Riding the Climate Wave: A Wavelet Analysis of Sovereign Risk in Developed and Developing Economies

Uke, O. D., Ben Amar, A., Gorelkina, O.

Under review at International Review of Financial Analysis (FINANA-D-25-05094); required reviews complete March 2026. Using wavelet coherence analysis on 59 countries (2008–2021), we find persistent long-term co-movement between sovereign CDS spreads and climate vulnerability, with bidirectional Toda-Yamamoto causality and no significant difference between advanced economies and EMDEs.

Working Paper

Climate Shocks, Sectoral Transmission, and Monetary Policy: Evidence from Food Manufacturing in the United Kingdom

Uke, O. D., Ben Amar, A., Gorelkina, O.

Presented at the 3rd QFDAM Conference, Rabat, June 2026. Target: Energy Economics. Using an eight-variable SVAR over 1990Q1–2025Q3, we show that food manufacturing contributes 1.4× more than agriculture to inflation forecast error variance, and that this contribution rose 13% between subsamples while agriculture's fell 93%.

Working Paper

Fiscal Capacity, Not Climate Exposure: Carbon Price Deficits and the Global Equity Burden of Fossil Fuel Subsidies

Uke, O. D., Ben Amar, A., Gorelkina, O.

Target: Energy Economics. We construct the first near-global panel of effective carbon prices for 174 countries (2015–2022). The average effective carbon price is −$68/tonne of CO₂. Normalized carbon price deficits exhibit a monotonic equity gradient driven by fiscal capacity rather than direct climate exposure, confirmed by bootstrapped mediation analysis (Imai-Keele-Yamamoto).

Building Resilience, Reducing Risk: A Systematic Review of Climate Resilience, Climate Finance Impacts, and Market Mispricing in the Global South
